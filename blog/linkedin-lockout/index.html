Greed cost me my LinkedIn account

I built a job-lead scraper that turned 428 LinkedIn leads into 75 applications in five days. Then I changed one number from 250 to 500. Here's how it worked, how well it worked, and how it got the account locked for good.

428leads found in four days

4humans replied within 48 hours
500the daily limit I set the morning it died

1.The machine

I'm job hunting, and I automate anything I do more than twice. Most of my pipeline reads job boards that publish their listings on purpose. The LinkedIn part was different: it drove a real browser, signed in to a second account, through a Chrome extension I wrote. Follow one lead through it:

Trace one lead (example data)


  


  
Every page load spent from a rolling budget, and a circuit breaker stopped everything at the first login wall. Simplified:


# per 15 minutes, per hour, per day
LIMITS = {"search": (3, 6, 12), "detail": (6, 12, 24)}

def can_open(kind, log, now):
    per_15, per_hour, per_day = LIMITS[kind]
    return (log.count(kind, since=now - 15 * MIN) < per_15
        and log.count(kind, since=now - HOUR) < per_hour
        and log.count(kind, since=now - DAY) < per_day)

if page.looks_like_checkpoint():
    breaker.trip("login_or_challenge")   # nothing runs until a human says so

  
Spot the bug I didn't. In my budget, one search counted as one. In the browser, one search paged through results 25 cards at a time and clicked the cards, and every click is a new URL. My budget counted what I meant. LinkedIn counted what the browser did.

A few things it did better than me scrolling at 1 a.m.:

  
    
    
  • Where to apply. It found the apply route for 88% of jobs: 144 Easy Apply, 178 on the company's own site (with the real link to its job board), 42 unknown.
    • 
    
  • Hiring posts, not just jobs. It searched posts too and pulled out 14 public "send your resume to" addresses. The good ones became email drafts in my Gmail with my resume attached, and I pressed Send myself.
    • 
    
  • Most of my week. 75 of the 113 applications I sent from 11 to 15 October came from it. That's two in three.
    • 
  

  
One honest catch: my hard rules auto-passed only 4 of the 225 jobs it read in full, because LinkedIn labels most roles just "India", with no word on remote. So every lead went through a second pass where my AI agent read the description for remote work, years and stack, and I made the call. 75 went in, 75 were ruled out (58 not a fit, 12 wrong place, 5 already applied), 6 had closed, and 24 are still waiting on me.

5.The appeal

  
Unlocking an account means proving who you are through Persona, LinkedIn's identity-verification partner. It never accepted any of my Indian IDs, and LinkedIn's help page says why: "Our verification system does not currently support the country that issued your identification document." India's DigiLocker option exists, but only for the profile badge in the mobile app.

Seventeen minutes from "please verify your ID" to "will remain restricted". Fair enough. LinkedIn's User Agreement forbids scraping and automated access, and allows one account per person. I broke both, and the rules won.
